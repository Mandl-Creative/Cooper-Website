This is the first in a series of conversations with people across the insurance world about how the work is actually shifting, and what they think is noise.

We are less interested in predictions than in what changed on a Tuesday. Below is a condensed version of the first conversation.

## On what actually moved in five years

The thing that changed was not placement. It was the volume of documentation around placement. Submissions carry more supporting material than they did, carriers ask for more of it up front, and the number of systems it has to pass through went up rather than down.

That is worth sitting with, because most of the technology sold into this market in that period promised the opposite.

## On what has not moved

Relationships with underwriters. The claim that distribution would disintermediate has been made for a long time and has not happened in commercial lines, because the hard accounts still get placed by someone picking up a phone to a person who trusts them.

The view here was blunt: anything that makes a broker faster at paperwork makes them better at that call, because they arrive at it with the work already done. Anything that tries to replace the call misunderstands what is being bought.

## On evaluating tools

The advice was to ignore the demo and ask what happens on a bad day. What does the tool do with a scanned loss run from a carrier that still faxes. What does it do when a portal changes. Who finds out first, and how.

A tool that is right most of the time and silent when it is wrong creates a new job, which is checking the tool.

## On what to watch

Not the model announcements. The last mile. Whoever makes filing genuinely reliable across the long tail of carrier portals will have done more for a broker's week than any improvement in drafting.
