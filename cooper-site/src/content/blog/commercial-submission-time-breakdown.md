Everyone in commercial lines has a number for this, and no two of them agree. So we followed one mid-market submission from intake to bound quote and timed every stage with a stopwatch rather than a memory.

It is one submission, not a study. But the shape of it matches what brokers describe, and the shape is the point.

## Where the hours went

The account was a light manufacturer, four locations, property and general liability, renewing with two incumbent markets and three new approaches.

Intake took the least time and produced the most rework. The client sent an updated schedule of values as a spreadsheet with merged cells and a vehicle list pasted into the notes column of a different tab.

Rebuilding the exposure schedule from that took over an hour. Chasing loss runs from two carriers took three days of waiting and about twenty minutes of actual work, spread across six emails. Filling the ACORD set took another hour and change. Portal entry for the three new markets took roughly forty minutes each, and one of them timed out twice.

Comparing the quotes when they came back took eleven minutes.

## The bottleneck is not judgement

Add it up and the judgement work, deciding what to place where and reading the quotes, is a small fraction of the total. The rest is moving structured information from one format into another format somebody specified years ago.

That is worth stating plainly because it decides what is worth automating. A tool that offers to help you decide is solving the eleven minutes. A tool that fills the schedule, retrieves the loss runs and completes the portal is solving the rest of the week.

## What the timing does not capture

Waiting. Three days of it, on loss runs, and it does not appear in any hours-worked number because nobody is working during it.

It is still the reason renewals feel late. The work is not slow; the work is interrupted, and every interruption costs the re-read that follows it. Any honest measurement of this process has to count the calendar, not only the clock.

We will run this again across more accounts and publish the spread.
