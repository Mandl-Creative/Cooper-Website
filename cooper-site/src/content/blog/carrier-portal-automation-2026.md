A tool that drafts a submission but cannot file it has moved the work, not removed it. The broker still logs in, still re-keys, still waits for the session to drop. The draft saved them the typing they were fastest at and left them the part they hate.

So the last mile is where broker time is actually won, and it is also where most of this category quietly stops.

## What works today

Portals with a real API work, and they are the minority. Where a carrier exposes one, filing is a solved problem and the only interesting questions are about mapping and error handling.

Portals with stable, conventional markup work well enough. The submission is completed by driving the form the way a person would, and the failure modes are the ordinary ones: a field that only appears after another is set, a validation rule that fires on blur, a dropdown whose options load late.

Neither of those is glamorous and both are reliable.

## What does not

Anything that changes without notice. A carrier can reorder two fields on a Tuesday and every automated submission to them fails on Wednesday, silently, in a way that looks like nothing happened at all.

Anything behind a bot check. That is a deliberate signal from the carrier, and driving around it is the wrong answer for a broker's compliance posture even where it is technically possible.

Anything requiring a document upload with a naming convention the portal does not state. These fail on the carrier's side, days later, and the first anyone knows is a request for a resubmission.

## How to evaluate a tool that claims this

Ask what happens on a bad day, not what the demo shows.

Which portals are covered, by name. What the system does when a portal changes: does it fail loudly, and who is told. Whether a failed filing is queued for a person or dropped. Whether the broker can see, for any given submission, exactly what was entered and where.

A tool that files nine out of ten submissions and says nothing about the tenth has not automated your last mile. It has added a checking job to your week and called it automation.

## Where this goes

The gap will close carrier by carrier, unevenly, and mostly through APIs rather than cleverness. Until then the honest position is a list of what is covered, published and kept current, rather than a claim about portals in general.
